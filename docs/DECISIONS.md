## Decided

**Tucked away, the toolbar icon shows "zz" and says so in its tooltip; there is no away timer, and drift words still come (2026-11-04, issue #87).**
Tested by hand in real Chrome: Muse's approval and its finish each came once while away, and Tuck stayed away through a full Chrome restart until "Bring Tuck back". The one thing missing was a sign that Tuck is away, so the icon carries "zz" (grey) and the tooltip "tucked away. Click to bring Tuck back." A word's own badge ("!" or "·") takes the place while the word is up, and "zz" comes back after it. No away timer ("for 1 hour", "until tomorrow"): a new day already brings Tuck back, and a timer is new UI for little gain; it can come after the hackathon. Drift words still come while away: tucking away hides Tuck, it does not stop it watching; do not disturb is for that.

**Deadlines are read as people write them, still only beside a cue, and a dated plan with no next action shows as upcoming (2026-11-04, issue #96).**
On a real brain, 11 of 338 open loops had a deadline, because only an ISO date beside one of 8 cue words counted. `due.js` now also reads "8 Nov", "Nov 8", "8 Kasım", "11 Aralık 2026" and ranges (the last day), a time with a zone on the person's own day, and the cues by, before, until, close, ends/ending and -e/-a kadar; the next action's deadline comes before the rest of the note's, and a cue followed by passed, missed, geçti or kaçırıldı does not count. On the same brain 34 loops now have one. The short cues count only as the word right before the date (and kadar only right after it), because "approved by Mira on 2026-11-06" sits next to "by" in every agent's note. A date with no year is the occurrence nearest the note's date, not the next one after it as the issue proposed: "the deadline was 8 Nov" in a note of 10 November would otherwise be a deadline a year out, and a wrong deadline is worse than a missing one. An entry with no next action and a cued date still ahead (9 on that brain) is listed in Coming up or Long run with its date and Hide; it is not in the task queue, so Tuck never says it and the task node never judges it.

**"I'm stuck" shows its step inline in the popup, and on the page in Tuck's drift bubble (2026-11-04, issue #61).**
The popup could hand the step off to Tuck on the tab in front, but Tuck cannot draw on chrome:// pages or a new tab, and the open popup covers the corner Tuck sits in, so the step shows in the "Working on" card with the same two buttons. In Tuck's drift bubble it sits next to "Take me back" and does not answer the word: the drift stays open until "Start this" takes the person back, which ends it as a return. The unsure question and the return word do not offer it. Asked for while Tuck is tucked away, the step still shows on that page and Tuck leaves with it, as with a drift word. A step comes late (the loop takes up to 20 s), so one check (`stuckWanted` in `tuck/present.js`) decides whether it is still wanted: the same ask (not closed, not replaced), the same goal, and the asking tab still in front; otherwise nothing is drawn, and Start this carries the goal id. One ask runs at a time. While a step or its wait is on the page in front, a drift word is held, as the away rule holds one: the engine's only holds are the dismiss cooldown, which still draws an L1 word, and the typing hold of the second look, and neither fits. Wording ("I'm stuck", "Start this", "Another one") and placement stay as built (agreed 2026-11-04). Tuck does not offer it on its own after a second drift: the button is on every drift word, and an offer is a new rule and a new model call before the demo. The labelled set of goals and the count of returns after Start this move to #111.

**"Your tasks" is Today, Missed, Coming up and Long run, and once the person has rows of their own, an agent's rows are a closed fold (2026-11-04, issue #92).**
On a real, busy memory a deadline missed two days ago led Today, and the person's own checkboxes sank among rows coding agents and the distill wrote. Today now holds the goal's row and what is due today or tomorrow (at most 5); a deadline passed 1 to 7 days ago is Missed and says "deadline passed N days ago"; Coming up is unchanged; what was "Everything else" is Long run. Once any row has `writer` own (a notes folder, a paste, `brain add` by hand), rows another writer made go under "Suggested from your memory", closed, with the same buttons; with no own rows nothing moves, so a brain-only memory reads as before. A row grouped from several entries counts as own if any of them is, since the person wrote it down too. The bubble still asks about a missed deadline within the week, but it shows the task's title first and says "Deadline passed 2 days ago" under it: "That was due …" put a pronoun before what it named. `scripts/replay-tasks.mjs` replays the list over a brain folder and stored verdicts and prints counts per view, never titles.

**The tool loop's notes carry their date and age, and a next action from before today is history (2026-11-04, issue #90).**
A plan could lead with "Review PR #12 and decide merge" (an invented example) two days after that PR was merged: the loop read a note's next action without its date, recall ranks by word match rather than time, and the plan moment could not see the day. `noteOf` (now `agent/notes.js`) adds an entry's date and its age by the person's local day ("today", "1 day ago"); a document has no date of its own and gets none. PLAN, STUCK and NEXT each say that a next action in a note from before today is used as a step only if nothing later (a newer note, `list_tasks`, today's pages) says it is done. The plan moment now also has `recent_activity`; `look_at_page` stays out of it, as before. No code filter drops such a step: telling "done" from "still open" across notes is the judgment the model is there for, and a word filter would drop real steps. The issue's optional distill change (no next action a page title contradicts) is left to `src/memory/distill.js`'s owner.

**The task node judges with the newest notes, from any topic, and leaves out upkeep chores and missed submissions (2026-11-04, issue #94).**
On a real, busy brain most kept rows were not to-dos: upkeep chores, things already done, duplicates and facts. The node saw the oldest 6 later notes on the entry's topic, so on a busy topic the note that closed a task was cut, and proof filed under another topic (the published post under `personal`, the task under `competition`) was never shown. It now takes the newest later notes on the topic first, then up to 4 notes whose titles share two words with the entry, a year not counting as a word: on its topic first, then ones written since on any other topic (an older one there is more often an earlier round of the same chore than proof). Each goes with its context and evidence (at most 120 characters each, redacted), where "this was done" is often written, and the entry goes with its own evidence line. New rules: an upkeep step that keeps a tool, pipeline or backup running is not a task, though fixing something that broke or leaked can be; a deadline the notes say has passed is not a task unless a later note commits to doing it anyway; money is personal only when it is the person's private finances, so a prize payout or the company's bank meeting is work. The example title "Decide whether to submit the grant application late" taught keeping a missed submission open and was replaced. A grouped row (#47) takes its deadline from the member its words come from, or the latest among its members, not the earliest. The rules changed, so every entry is judged once again.

**Whether a PR or issue a step names is merged or closed is looked up on GitHub, in code, before the step is shown (2026-11-04, issue #91).**
Notes go stale about code: a plan led with "Review PR #41" two days after it merged, and only the live repository knew. Settings takes a list of public repositories (empty by default, and then GitHub is never asked). The agent loop gets a read tool, `github_status`, for states and titles; separately, before a plan step, a stuck step or the goal-done line is shown, code finds `#N`, `PR N`, `issue N` and `owner/name#N` in it and leaves the step out if GitHub says merged or closed, writing it in the run's trail. The check is code, not the model, because a merge is a fact (INTENT: facts are deterministic). A bare `#N` counts only when one repository is listed; a repository not listed is never asked about. GitHub's unauthenticated API allows 60 requests an hour, so answers are cached for 5 minutes in session storage, and a 403 or 429 stops asking for 5 minutes; a failed lookup keeps the step, since a missed stale step costs less than a dropped live one. Task rows in Settings are not checked yet (#92 was reworking that list), and private repositories need a token: later work.

**The task node reads the whole day's notes with their results, and the day's browsing as evidence that a task is done (2026-11-04, issues #75, #76).**
A row stayed after its work was under way because the node saw titles only, of an arbitrary slice of the same day's notes, and nothing of the day. Entries carry no time, so every note from the entry's own day now goes as possibly later, with its date and result line (at most 150 characters, redacted); the caps stay. One summary of the last 24 hours of browsing (at most 6 sites, 2 titles each) goes once per run, without any site the never-read list or the bank, vault and password rule keeps from being read. It may only close a task, never make one, and it is not in a verdict's hash, so the day going on asks nothing again. Batches go 10 at a time, not 15: at 15 the slowest eval batch took 29.7 s against the worker's 30-second fetch limit. A planning or "decide how to" step is closed when later notes or the browsing show the work being done. A title names what the action is for, from the entry's title or context when the next action leaves it out.

**At the moments the engine or the person picks, a tool loop decides what Tuck says and offers (2026-11-04, issues #81, #78).**
Each model call was a fixed step chosen in code, so nothing in Tuck looked anything up. At a goal set, a goal done and "I'm stuck", the model now calls read tools (memory's search, the to-do list, the last 30 minutes of pages, the last work page's excerpt, the goal) and ends with one finishing tool whose arguments are checked in code: in Tuck's voice, not generic, and built from the goal or what was read. When Tuck speaks stays deterministic: the loop runs only at those moments, never per page, since a per-page loop would be slow and Chrome may stop a worker's fetch at 30 s. It has 5 model calls and 20 s at most, and runs outside the input queue, which #67 showed holds every other input back. So no moment waits for it: a plan lands on the goal when it comes, the goal-done line is written while the goal runs and used only if ready, and nothing is redrawn later, since that would wipe a goal being typed. Finishing tools only propose; acting takes the person's tap. #78 asked for a single call with no tools. The plan is that loop's first moment instead, so #61 and #78 share one node. A plan needs notes the loop actually read: with none, there is no plan rather than a generic one.

**The to-do list holds the person's commitments, not a coding agent's steps, and an old distilled loop is not offered (2026-11-04, issues #75, #76).**
On a real brain the list read like a git log ("Merge PR #39", "Run npm run eval"): most open loops there are notes coding agents wrote at the end of a session, and the old prompt kept any action someone "or their team" had to do. The node now keeps what a person would write on their own list, and a step that is the only trace of something larger is listed as that larger thing ("Ship the Linux bridge"), with one match key so the steps fold into one row (#47). It is told each entry's age and who wrote it: the person, a coding agent (a brain `source` other than `manual`) or a distill job (`brain-distill`, the extension's `distill`). Code ranks the person's own first within a deadline and among the undated, and leaves out a distilled loop older than 14 days unless its deadline is live, because the distill job restates work that comes up again; 14 is the queue's existing `staleAfter`, and on the brain measured, 66 of its 71 distilled loops were 14 days old or less. An agent's own note is not cut by age, because it can hold a real commitment ("email the deck by the 8th"); the node judges it. Verdicts are asked again when the rules change or an entry ages past a week and a month.

**Tuck's words about Muse come from the model, and the bridge adds the folder's name and an approval's tool (2026-11-04, issue #67).**
The fixed lines said the same thing every time and carried nothing. The bridge now also passes the last part of the hook's `cwd`, minutes since the turn's prompt by its own clock, and, for an approval only, the name of the tool last about to run (`PreToolUse`, newly subscribed; the hook keeps the name and drops the input). These say where and how long, never what: no prompt, tool input, message or output leaves, as before. Measured live at low effort (Muse Spark 1.3, 8 calls), a line takes 5.9 to 14.6 s. The issue proposed waiting up to 3 s for it, but the wait ran in the worker's input queue: the e2e failed 3 of 3 runs, because drift words queued behind the agent word replaced it as soon as it was drawn, and 3 s would almost never be enough anyway. So no word waits: it is the model's when its line was written ahead, otherwise the template is said at once, now with the same facts. Lines are written ahead where the moment is known: a turn's end when the bridge starts holding it (3.5 to 15 s), and the ask again when the first wait is said. A first wait, with nothing ahead of it, is always the template.

**A Chrome restart is not a new day: the day's greeting comes once (2026-11-04, Tuck v1 skins).**
The engine lives in session storage, which a restart wipes, so `sessionDay` went back to null and the first page with no goal greeted again: after a restart, a Done brought "Hi. What are we working on today?" back, and the greeting also undid Tuck away. `sessionDay` is now kept in local storage too and given back with the mode and the goal. This sits beside #73 (a finished goal counts as the day started): #73 keeps the greeting from replacing a Done's word, this keeps any restart from being a new day; both hold after the merge.

**Tuck's focus ring is in the ink colour, not the accent (2026-11-04, Tuck v1 skins).**
The skins spec asked for an accent focus ring on the bubble's buttons, inputs, × and Tuck itself. The v1 accent is mint (#94BFAE), and mint on the cream surface is about 1.9:1, below DESIGN.md's 3:1 floor for focus rings, so a keyboard user could lose their place. The ring uses `--tk-ink` instead, which every skin's theme has and which clears the floor on its own surface; the accent stays as the primary button's fill.

**A page for another of the person's commitments is not drift, and the log says what each call was sent, as counts (2026-11-03, issue #8).**
Time on the tax form while the goal is the avatar is not a distraction to talk someone back from, and calling it unrelated is the false warning INTENT counts against Tuck. So `other_commitment` is a label of its own: it holds the drift budget like neutral, gets no word, and is never a page to go back to. The model can only find it when it knows the other commitments, so the to-do list's first 5 open titles go in the prompt, redacted, to a model that does not train on them; with an empty list the rule and the list stay out and the prompt is unchanged. Each model call's log record now has `sent`: how many examples, notes and commitments it got, and whether a profile and a conversation heading went with it. The texts stay out, as the excerpt and the model's reason already did, because the log is exported and recordings of it are committed. Measured on a 15-minute recording (`test/fixtures/2026-11-03-avatar`): no warning in 13 minutes of work, both drifts caught, 46 s and 42 s into them; `npm run eval` 98.0% accurate, false "unrelated" 5.0% (one of three runs of `shop-gh-pr`).

**While Muse waits, Tuck asks again, and only asks (2026-11-02, issue #11).**
One command can ask many times on one hook (its stages), and no hook marks an approval being answered: `PreToolUse` fires before `PermissionRequest`, and the next event is `PostToolUse`, once the tool has run. So Tuck cannot tell "still waiting" from "approved and running". One word per status left the person browsing while Muse waited. Tuck now asks again after 30 s in the browser, then 60, 120 and so on; time in the terminal does not count. The wording is a question because it can be wrong, and the gaps grow because a wrong question must stay cheap. Tuck counts drift as usual meanwhile: time on an unrelated page after an approval may still be a wait, which remains an open limit.

**"Muse is done" waits for Muse's answer to be on screen (2026-11-02, issue #11).**
Muse fires `Stop`, runs its internal helpers, and only then shows the answer: 3.2 s and 5.9 s later in two timed headless runs, with helpers up to 2.7 s apart. Tuck said "done" while the person still saw it working. No hook marks the answer being shown, so the bridge holds the end of a turn until hooks from every session have been quiet for 3.5 s, 15 s at most, so the word can trail the answer by that much. An approval goes at once, since Muse shows it already. The wait lives in the bridge: it is about Muse's timing, and the engine stays unaware of it.

**One drift stays one drift, and words are spaced by time off task (2026-11-02, issue #37).**
A recorded session showed two faults. Time waiting for the model never counted, so hopping faster than it answers looked like no drift at all. And any glance away ended the episode while the budget stayed high, so the next page fired a new L1 at once: eight words in 5.5 minutes, none past L1. The wait now counts once the model says unrelated, and the next word needs `escalateAfterSec` more seconds off task, whatever the clock says, because a glance or time in a terminal says nothing about drifting further. A drift ends on a real absence or once work has drained the budget well below the tolerance. Quiet after the last word holds for that page only: a new page starts over, or hopping would end in silence. A page still waiting counts as neutral, so no word is ever said about a page not yet judged.

**A small local bridge tells Tuck whose turn it is in Muse Code, status only (2026-11-02, issue #11).**
While an agent works, time on another page is a wait, not drift, and when it stops the person should hear it where they are. A browser extension cannot hear a hook, so this is the one exception to "no separate backend": the hook appends a status line to a file, and a native messaging host Chrome starts passes it on, so the hook needs nothing running. Prompts, messages and terminal output never leave the hook; whether a turn ended in a question is worked out there. Tuck cannot bring a terminal to the front, so the word offers "Got it", not "Take me back".

**The tab-hopping nudge is off by default, and counts only time the model was sure about (2026-11-02, issue #30).**
It fired during real work: six tabs in two minutes is normal (docs, repo, chat, a PR), and the pattern looks the same whether someone is working or not, so the nudge read as a false accusation. Sustained off-task time already reaches the drift nudge in about 45 s, whatever the switch count; what is lost is quick hopping between work and off-task tabs, where the work tabs keep draining the budget. Behind `fragmentation: true` it still works, now counting only confident "unrelated" time and with wording about the tabs, not the switching. The thresholds (6 switches, 50%) are unchanged: they are guesses, and changing them without replaying real sessions would swap one guess for another.

**The to-do list is read from the open loops by a model at import; what is urgent stays in code (2026-11-02, issue #29).**
Memory counts every entry with a next action as an open loop, and on a real brain that was 225 items, 9 of them todos. Rules on kind or age cannot tell "send the papers" from "keep an eye on the bill", or see that a later note closed a task, so a model reads each candidate once, with the other notes on its topic, and says whether it is a task, still open, and its title in 10 words. It never orders them: the deadline is read from the text and the queue keeps memory's order, as INTENT.md asks. Verdicts are cached by entry and content, so a re-import asks only about what changed (on that brain, 15 calls the first time, then none). A task closed on another topic, or never written down, stays listed; Not a task and Done take it out for good.

**"Take me back" teaches only as the answer to Tuck's question; labels can be forgotten in settings (2026-11-01, issue #8).**
Counting every "Take me back" was the plan, but the button only appears on a page already judged unrelated, so it adds little, and a page wrongly marked as work never gets a nudge to answer. So a no on a page Tuck was unsure about is remembered at once, for that page's title or that conversation, never the whole site: a repo on the same host can be the work. Settings list what was learned. A later yes takes the no back. Forgetting a label does not remove a preference already written to memory, since a lasting label writes one and `src/memory/` has no way to remove it yet.

**Reminders name what is due at moments that already exist, in a fixed line (2026-11-01, issue #10).**
Urgency changes what Tuck says, never how often it interrupts: a greeting, a return, a break's end and a finished goal, once per task per day. The notes have no way to mark a task done, so "done" is kept in the extension by entry id. A line a template can write needs no model call.

**Notes reach the page decision through memory's search, with no second form (2026-11-01, issues #8, #6).**
The notes are already distilled, so a facts layer on top of them would repeat that work. The canonical form is their own record; other sources become entries once, at import, in #6. The search finds candidates in code, with up to 5 notes going to the model for that page or person: the model is stateless, so sending every note means sending them all on every call, and the search alone also matches another Ali or a "Create post" dialog. It costs one model call per page or person with matching notes, and a replay cannot show two memories, so that scene is tested end to end.

**Tuck notes which conversation the person last touched, by its heading, not only before a nudge (2026-11-01, issue #9).**
Notes should decide what a page means: a conversation with someone the person works with is work, and the feed on the same URL is not. That needs the name, immediately and with no model call, and opening a chat fires no Chrome event. So a listener notes the last container touched (a dialog with a text box, or the main area on a messaging URL); touching anything else ends it, because the focus stays in the chat's box while the feed scrolls (seen on real LinkedIn). Only the heading is read, since LinkedIn's labels hold message text. It goes to the model only when the person's notes mention that name, and is not read on never-read sites or with reading off. Keeping it is deliberate, the one piece of page text that is kept: in the event log for 7 days, in verdict and label keys, and in the preference memory gets once a conversation's label lasts, which the export also writes to the brain inbox. A verdict or label per person needs the name, and everything stays local unless the person connects a brain.

**Before a drift word, Tuck looks at the page again: typing, then a short excerpt (2026-11-01, issue #5).**
URL and title cannot tell a work chat from a feed on the same site: Safa was nudged while messaging a teammate about this project in LinkedIn's chat window. Typing is a yes/no checked before every drift word: focus in a text field and a key pressed in the last minute (only that time is noted). The excerpt is read a little ahead because it takes a model call: up to 1,000 characters from where the person types, else from the middle of the window. It is redacted, sent to a model that does not train on it, and only the verdict is kept. It rules out screenshots and site-specific scraping, and it never runs on pages with a password field or on the never-read sites.

**"This is for my task" on a whole site lasts 30 minutes; for good only after 3 different days (2026-11-01, issue #5).**
One click meant "this visit is work", but it made the whole site work forever, which hides the next real drift there (the feed after the chat). Title labels (YouTube and the like) still last.

**One commitment written in several notes is one row in the to-do list, and the entries stay as they are (2026-11-03, issue #47).**
A note restated on a later day, or the same task in the brain, a notes folder and a pasted memory, listed the task several times, and Done on one row left the others open. Code picks the pairs that share most of their words (about 100 of 26,000 on one real brain), and the model says of each whether it is one piece of work. The grouping is a view over the entries, so nothing in anyone's notes is rewritten or deleted. A pair the model called different, or the person split with Not the same, is never merged, not even through a third entry, because a wrong merge hides a task, which is worse than a task listed twice.
